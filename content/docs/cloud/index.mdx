Yukibana Cloud, at [cloud.yukibana.dev](https://cloud.yukibana.dev), is where
exercises are handed out and handed in. It is deliberately a **registry**: it
stores what it is given and transforms nothing.

- A teacher publishes a **release** of a project: two archives built from the
  course repository by the [CLI](/docs/reference/cli), on their machine or in the
  repository's CI through the [GitHub Action](/docs/reference/github-action).
  The **starter** has the hidden tests removed; the **teacher archive** is the
  whole project, for staff.
- Students download the starter while the project's window is open, and
  submit their solution as a `.tar.gz`, late if after the deadline.
- Staff read the submissions, and download them **assembled**: with the
  read-only and hidden files put back, and every tampered file reported.

## What it does not do

Some things are left out on purpose, at least for now:

- **It never reads a repository.** An earlier design built starters inside the
  cloud on every push, through a GitHub App. That needed a paid hosting plan for
  the CPU time, a webhook surface, and a copy of every teacher's repository.
  Building in the CLI, where the repository already is, removed all three.
- **It never runs student code.**
- **No grading, feedback, plagiarism checks, team submissions or per-student
  extensions yet**, and no self-service joining or invitation emails:
  enrolment is silent, and the course is simply there at the student's first
  login. These are designed to be added later without fighting what exists,
  together with roster and grading integrations such as Moodle.

## Logging in

Identity is a verified email address. People log in with **GitHub**, or with a
**one-time code** sent to their address; there are no passwords. Enrolment
matches on the address, so a student whose GitHub address differs from their
school address simply uses the code. The CLI and the IDE log in through the
website, as the account the person confirms there. CI publishes with a
per-project token instead.

## Live pages

Open pages update by themselves: when a project, a release, the roster or a
submission changes, everyone looking at that edition sees it without reloading.

## Privacy and authorisation

Every rule about who may see or do what lives in the database's row-level
policies, and every request runs as the person making it, so the website, the
CLI and the IDE cannot see more than the policies allow. A student never sees
another person's address; staff see the roster of their own editions only.
Submissions are append-only: there is no way to change or delete one.
