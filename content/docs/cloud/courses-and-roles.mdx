## The model

**Course → edition → project → submission.**

- A **course** is a code that groups its editions.
- An **edition** is one run of the course, a semester or a year. It carries the
  course's name as it ran then, its roster, and its projects. A new edition is
  a **copy** of the previous one: projects and staff come along, students and
  dates do not.
- A **project** is one exercise in an edition, with its releases and its dates.
  Staff order an edition's projects by dragging them.
- A **submission** is one version of a student's work on a project.

Reusing an assignment next year means duplicating the edition rather than
sharing the project, so each year's staff only ever see their own projects,
hidden-test configuration included.

## Roles

There are two kinds of role.

**Platform roles**, per account:

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| `user` | Everything their enrolments allow. |
| `teacher` | Also create courses and editions. |
| `admin` | Also make teachers. An admin is not implicitly staff anywhere: to see an edition, they enrol in it. |

**Edition roles**, per enrolment:

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| `student` | See the edition's open projects, download their starters, submit, and read their own submissions. |
| `assistant` | Staff: create, edit, reorder projects and publish releases; read every submission. |
| `owner` | Everything an assistant can, plus run the edition: the roster and roles, duplicating and archiving, deleting projects, and making publishing tokens. |

Pages only show the controls your role can use.

## Enrolment

Staff enrol people **by email address**, before or after that person has ever
logged in. An address that already belongs to an account links at once; an
unknown address links at that person's first confirmed login. The roster shows
who has and has not linked yet. Removing someone is the same whether or not
they ever logged in.

From the terminal:

```bash
yukibana enrol <edition-id> alice@school.example bob@school.example
yukibana members <edition-id>
yukibana unenrol <edition-id> bob@school.example
```

## A project's dates

| Date | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Available after** | The project opens to students. Unset: a draft only staff see. |
| **Deadline** | When it is due. Work after it is accepted and marked late. |
| **Closes** | The project disappears for students and stops accepting work. Unset: never. |

Lateness is computed when it is read, from the time the server stamped on the
submission, so extending a deadline makes earlier late work on time. Students
keep access to their own submissions after a project closes.

The student's **To do** tab lists, across every edition, what is still to hand
in (soonest deadline first, overdue on top) and what is handed in.
